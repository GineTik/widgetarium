Widgetarium UI Kit

Built from the references, not from what the plugin happens to draw today. Filled surfaces, no borders. Every button a capsule. Grey plus one accent, and three status colours that mean something. Colours, type and spacing come from the vault's own theme by their real Obsidian names; only the two large radii, the cell and the motion are ours, because Obsidian has no word for them.

Layer 2 — the kit

Surfaces, and how one becomes a sheet

Press a tile. The sheet does not appear over it — it is the tile, grown: the panel starts at that tile's exact rectangle and radius and springs out to full size, then collapses back into it. Nothing fades in from the middle of the screen.

The dialog casts no shadow · measured, not preferred

The panel above used to sit on --wg-shadow-l. Sampled over the overlay's scrim it read (227, 227, 229) against its own (240, 240, 240) plate — a raised surface rendering darker than the thing it sits on, which is elevation backwards. It is an opaque raised fill with a hairline edge now, measured at (255, 255, 255) over 240.

Glass was rejected here specifically. A blur needs something behind it worth blurring, and a uniform scrim gives it nothing — the same result the four beds at the bottom of this page show. So the dialog takes the fourth surface instead: --wg-raise, white mixed into the background, which is a no-op in light and a real step in dark. This is the kit's own law applied, not an exception written for the dialog: a floating surface is told apart by its edge, never by a cast shadow, and --wg-shadow-l no longer exists.

Layer 2 — the kit

Popover — the control becomes the surface

Not a dialog. A dialog is its own thing, centred, over a scrim. A popover is the control you pressed: it leaves, the panel takes its exact rectangle, and grows only as far as its own content needs — staying where the control stood. The same mechanism drives a menu, a select and a whole tile, because the trigger is just an element.

Placement · two origins, one panel

The fold above is over, and it is still the default: the trigger leaves and the panel takes its exact rectangle. below is the second origin — the panel hangs under a trigger that stays on screen, six pixels down, and flips above itself when the bottom of the window is too close. Press both. The difference is the whole point, and it is not a difference a screenshot can carry.

over · the control becomes the panel
below · the control stays, the panel hangs off it 
<Popover placement="below" trigger={<Button>{value}</Button>}>
	{options.map((name) => (
		<PopoverItem checked={name === value} onClick={() => pick(name)}>{name}</PopoverItem>
	))}
</Popover>

A below panel is at least as wide as its trigger. Only the kit has measured that trigger, so it hands the number down as --wg-anchor-width and the stylesheet reads it as a min-width — a width offered, not forced, so a panel whose contents need more still takes more. Both placements fold out of, and back into, the trigger's rectangle: the origin changes, the motion does not.

A keyword field in the panel · PopoverSearch

When a menu is long enough that reading it is work, the panel grows a field at the top. It narrows the choices under it, never the board — nothing outside the panel notices it was typed in, and it empties when the panel closes.

Press it, then type

The component owns one thing: the field, and the trimmed lower-case needle it hands down. Who counts as a match is the caller's rule — a name here, but a path, a tag, an alias or a fuzzy score somewhere else, and none of that belongs in a kit part. The hint line under the field is the caller's too.

<Popover placement="below" trigger={trigger}>
	<PopoverSearch placeholder="Find a person" hint="Name or initials">
		{(needle) => people
			.filter((one) => one.name.toLowerCase().includes(needle))
			.map((one) => <PopoverItem key={one.id}>{one.name}</PopoverItem>)}
	</PopoverSearch>
</Popover>
Layer 2 — the kit

Calendar — the seam is the day’s content

The kit owns the month, the six-row grid that keeps the panel one height all year, and the three states a cell can be in — outside, today, selected. It does not own what the cell says. renderDay({date, outside, today, selected}) returns the cell’s children, so a caller can hang anything off a day without the kit learning a word about deadlines, habits or bookings.

No renderDay · the date, which is the default
renderDay · a number and the day’s dots
renderDay · roman numerals, to prove the seam is real

Press any day in any of the three. The middle one is the reason the seam exists: the dots are the caller’s, drawn from the caller’s own data, and the kit never learns they are there. The third is deliberately silly — if roman numerals fit through the same hole, so does anything.

<Calendar
	selected={due}
	onSelect={setDue}
	renderDay={({ date, today }) => (
		<>
			{date.getDate()}
			<span class="cal-dots">{dotsFor(date).map(() => <i />)}</span>
		</>
	)}
/>
And the shortcuts are not a calendar feature

“Today” and “Next Monday” account for most dates anybody sets, and they are ordinary PopoverItems under a PopoverSeparator — composed at the call site, in the panel, beside the calendar. Not a shortcuts prop, not a showToday boolean. That composability is the thing being demonstrated: the next screen that needs “End of sprint” adds one more button and asks the kit for nothing.

<Popover placement="below" trigger={<Row>{shown}</Row>}>
	<Calendar selected={due} onSelect={setDue} />
	<PopoverSeparator />
	<PopoverItem onClick={() => setDue(today)}>Today</PopoverItem>
	<PopoverItem onClick={() => setDue(nextMonday)}>Next Monday</PopoverItem>
</Popover>
Layer 2 — the kit

Markdown editor — raw text, made readable

This is Detail mode, one half of a pair. Preview is Obsidian’s own rendering — read mode, plugins’ output included, and not ours to reimplement. Detail is the raw note in a plain text box with every marker still on screen: hashtags stay hashtags, [[links]] keep their brackets, **bold** keeps its asterisks. Weight and colour do all the work and nothing moves, hides or re-wraps. It is deliberately not live preview.

Type in it. Links take the accent, headings and bold markup go bold, italics go italic, and inline code is left plain on purpose — colouring it would be a fourth rule nobody asked for, and a backticked span is consumed first precisely so the markers inside it stay ordinary text. A hashtag is left alone for the same reason: the tags have their own row elsewhere.

<MarkdownEditor value={body} onInput={setBody} placeholder="Notes" />

The implementation is a transparent textarea over a styled mirror. There are no elements inside a <textarea>, only characters, so coloured-but-plain text is always either this or a contenteditable. The mirror holds the spans and the colour; the textarea sits on top with color: transparent and a visible caret, and native editing — selection, undo, spellcheck, the OS’s own text menu — is kept intact. The cost is that the two must agree on every metric that decides where a character lands, so padding, font, line-height, letter-spacing, wrapping and tab-size are declared once on .md-text and inherited by both. They also share one scroller, so there is no pair of scroll offsets to keep in step.
