Widgetarium proposal · 29 August 2026

Widget settings

Today the panel is position: absolute; inset: 0 over the tile — it covers the widget you are configuring (styles.css:564). This proposes the opposite. Press settings and the widget itself grows out of its tile into a window that is all playground: a white canvas carrying the board's grid, running edge to edge. The header and the settings are two floating glass panels over that canvas, and the widget pans underneath them. One surface, two panels, no regions, and nothing casts a shadow.

The motion

The tile becomes the window

Not a dialog on an overlay. The same tile element is given a bigger box and animated into it, so the widget inside is never unmounted and never re‑created — it keeps the rows it had fetched, the column it was scrolled to and the popover it had open. The board dims behind it; nothing else moves.

1 · At rest

Editing mode. The tile carries two controls in its corner — settings and remove — exactly the pair surface.js draws today.

2 · The press

The gear answers under the finger — scale(0.955) over --wg-dur-press. Nothing else has happened yet.

3 · Growing

Mid‑flight, at roughly 45%. The widget is still the widget — it is re‑laying itself out into a wider box, not being stretched by a scale.

4 · Landed

The box has stopped, and the widget has been placed at the scale that fits the free area. Only now do the panels fade in — opacity on --wg-ease, delayed 80 ms, the beat the kit's popover uses for .pop-inner.

5 · Back

The panels fade first, then the box returns on --wg-ease, not the spring. The kit's rule: a panel folding away that bounces reads as indecision.

The tile's own box transitionreused

styles.css:287 already transitions a tile's transform, width and height. That is the whole mechanism — the settings grow is the same transition given a bigger target. Two changes proposed: 320 ms on --wg-spring instead of 170 ms on a private curve, because this box travels much further than a resize does and should overshoot once on the way out.

Where it landsreused

chip.js already answers this for a too‑narrow tile: openedBox() centres the target on the tile it grew from and pushes it back inside the board with an 8 px margin, so a tile against the left wall opens to the right. The settings window wants a bigger wantedBox() — nearly the whole board — and nothing else changes.

Above the dimreused

.wg-tile-chip.is-open takes z-index: 30, above the board's scrim and above every tile it now covers (styles.css:819). The settings window is the same state under a different name. The dim is the board's, not the app's — the note around it stays readable and scrollable.

Not the kit's popover FLIP, and this decides more than the motion. The kit grows a popover by scaling it from the trigger's rectangle (showPop, with the per‑axis radius correction in startRadius). Right for a menu, wrong here: a transformed ancestor silently re‑parents every position: fixed descendant to itself and kills backdrop-filter — the kit's own source says so, above placePop. In this window that breaks both: the widget's popovers, and the glass on the two floating panels. So the window animates its box, and the same rule decides how the canvas zooms.

Press the gear — the tile below really does travel; nothing is swapped out

The four tiles are one DOM element each. Opening changes one element's width, height and transform — the widget inside it is the same node the whole way. That is the claim the design rests on, and it is cheap because it is what the board already does.

The arrangement

One surface, two floating panels

The playground is not a column beside a sidebar — it is the whole window: a white canvas carrying the board's own grid at the board's own cell size (54 px cells, 12 px gaps, from paths.js GRID). The header hangs over it. The settings hang over it. The grid does not stop where a panel begins. Shown in its opening state: the widget has landed whole inside the free area, clear of both panels, and the person takes over from there. Mid‑change, too: Columns is open — the panel has taken the row's place, as the kit's popover does — and ticking Review has put a fourth column in the widget behind it.

Every group is a grouped listthe kit's

The heading sits outside the block, small and muted, as macOS System Settings does it — so the block holds rows and nothing else, and there is no heading in there to divide from. The footnote goes outside too. Inside, a hairline appears only between rows of a genuine list: the four abilities, the filters, the views. The header, against the macOS onewhat changed

macOS puts the blur behind a full‑width title and toolbar pinned to the window's top edge, and it earns it because content scrolls up past it on one axis. Here the canvas moves on two axes and has no top edge to scroll from, so a full‑width bar would subtract a band across the entire window from the free area on every open. The capsule does the same job for less: content still passes under it — drawn in the glass section, a column going beneath it — and the blur still separates the title from what is travelling behind. The material is unchanged; only the width is. If you want the macOS proportion, widening it back is one property and costs about 40 px of canvas height.

One panel, because one edgethe choice

The settings could equally be two or three floating groups. They are one panel because the only legitimate edge on this window is the hairline that stops a translucent surface dissolving into the moving content behind it — and every extra floating thing would need its own. One panel means one edge, and everything inside it separated by fill: the panel's material, then a group's fill on it, then rows that are not surfaces at all and carry a single hairline between them.

Remove is not in this windowa call

The tile keeps both controls; the header keeps only Done. Removing acts on the board, not on the configuration, and a bin beside a close control in one small capsule is the one mis‑press you cannot undo. If it must be here it goes alone at the foot of the last tab.

The opening position

The fit is measured against the free area, not the window

When the window opens, the scale is chosen so the whole widget lands inside the part of the canvas that is actually visible — left of the sidebar, below the header — without sliding under either. Measuring against the whole window instead would tuck part of the widget under a panel the instant it appeared, which is the one thing the opening state must not do. From there the person takes over with the zoom controls.

THE OPENING SCALE free = window − the header's rectangle − the sidebar's rectangle − the zoom bar's band − one cell of margin fit = min(1, free.width / widget.width, free.height / widget.height) open = max(fit, 0.7) if open > fit the widget is larger than the floor allows: do not zoom past it. start panned to the widget's top-left corner, inside free, and say so in the bar. folding the sidebar away grows free, so Fit recomputes and the number in the bar moves. 0.7 is a legibility floor, not a taste: the smallest type the kit authors is 12px, and at 0.7 that renders at 8.4px — the point below which the picture stops answering "does this look right".

When it already fits, fit is 1 and the window opens at 1:1 — which is most desktop cases, including the 12×8 kanban above. So the rule has no cost in the common case: it only bites when the widget is genuinely larger than the space, and then it does the arithmetic the person would otherwise do by hand.

The floor is the honest part. A 20‑column widget is 1308 px wide; in a 850 px free area that is 0.65, and at 0.65 the card titles are 7.8 px. Zooming there would produce a picture nobody can judge and call it a preview. Starting panned at 0.7 admits the truth: this widget does not fit, here is its top left, move around it.

Keeping the settings readable over content that is moving is the whole job.

The blur is the part that earns its placeover content

blur(24px) saturate(180%) turns a passing card into a wash instead of a razor line of unrelated contrast crossing your settings. The saturation is what keeps a red P1 reading as a warm smear rather than as grey mud, so the panel still tells you something is under here and it is moving — which is the feedback that makes panning legible.

The tint is a no‑op in light, a real step in darkarithmetic

--wg-glass-panel is color-mix(--wg-raise 78%, transparent), and --wg-raise is #ffffff 12% over the note's background. In a light vault that is white into white — deliberately, because the raise exists to lift a panel in dark. So in dark the sidebar visibly rises off the canvas with nothing added; in light it does not, and the next card is why that is survivable.

The hairline never failsany theme

--wg-glass-edge is inset 0 0 0 1px of the ink at 12%, not of a background colour. An ink step survives a white canvas, a dark one and a themed one alike, which is why it is the one part of the recipe that holds everywhere — and why no drop shadow is needed to make a panel read as floating.

One panel, and the groups inside it are structure — which needs a fill the kit did not have. A grouped list normally takes --wg-fill, a small step from --background-primary. Inside a translucent panel there is no solid background to step from: over a white canvas the panel and the fill are both white, the group loses its edge against the panel, and what is left is three cards floating independently — which is the thing that stopped it reading as our system. So a group on glass steps toward the ink instead, from the panel's own raised colour: --wg-glass-group = color-mix(--wg-ink 6%, --wg-raise). Darker in light, lighter in dark, a constant step either way, and opaque, because it carries text and a 22% ghost of a passing card behind an 11 px label is dirty (a 78% tint over a glyph is about #c7c7c7, 1.55:1). The panel keeps its real job — the frame and the gutters between the groups, where the widget shows through and tells you it is still moving under there.

One edge on the whole window, and no shadow anywhere. The kit's first law is fill, never outline — a surface is told apart by what it is filled with, and a line only ever divides two rows of one group. So the panel carries --wg-glass-edge and nothing inside it carries a second one: the group is a different fill on the panel, a row is not a surface at all, and the only line in the sidebar is the hairline between two rows. A widget on the canvas is told from the canvas by the grid it covers, not by a ring. Where an earlier draft of this page reached for a border, the honest reading was that one of the two surfaces had the wrong fill — and that is what got fixed, not the border weight.

Panning

The widget slides under, but it cannot hide

Part of the widget going under a panel is not a defect — it is how you reach the far edge of something wider than the free area, and it is the state the blur exists for. What would be a defect is losing it. So the pan is clamped, and the clamp is stated against the panels rather than against the window.

Fitting a large widget

Three ways, and one of them breaks the widget

You gave three, and leaned toward the third. All three are drawn, because the reason to reject the first two is not taste in either case.

1 · Shrink to its minimumShows the wrong thing

Not a risk — a certainty, and the code says so. The kanban declares collapseBelowPx: 240, and below that width the board does not draw a small kanban, it draws a chip (chip.js). "Shrink it to its minimum" therefore previews the widget refusing to be itself. It survives as the Narrow button: a deliberate check on the chip state, never a default.

Two costs, and the second is not about legibility: a transformed ancestor re‑parents every position: fixed descendant to itself and disables backdrop-filter. In this window that hits twice — the widget's popovers land wrong, and the two floating panels stop being glass. At 0.55 a 42 px control is also 23 px, under any published touch target.

3 · Pan and zoom, like a canvasRecommended

1:1

The widget keeps its real size and the person moves around it. Right — but only with the rule below attached, because a zoom is a transform and inherits option 2's defect at every zoom except one.

Take 3, arrive at the free‑area fit, and let 1 and 2 be buttons rather than modes — your combination of 1 and 3, held apart by one rule. While the zoom is not 1:1 the canvas is look‑only: a press pans, and a press on the widget snaps to 1:1 centred on what you touched. So the widget is only ever interactive at zoom 1, where no transform exists, where its popovers land correctly and where the glass is still glass. Everything below 1:1 is a picture you are reading, not a thing you are using — which is exactly what an arrival state should be. Settings

What changes, and what does notphone

The free area is measured the same way — window minus the header, minus the sheet at its peek height. So the arrival rule is unchanged; only the rectangle it subtracts moves from the right edge to the bottom.

The clamp turns vertical. One cell must stay in the band between the header and the peek line. Dragging the sheet up is allowed to cover the widget completely: that is a gesture you are holding, and letting go returns it.

The zoom bar rides on the sheet's edge and moves with it, so the two things that want the bottom‑left corner never collide.

A phone is where the blur matters most and costs most. The screen is small, so the widget is nearly always under the sheet — the case the blur exists for. It is also where backdrop-filter is most expensive to composite. If a frame budget ever forces the choice, drop the blur and keep the hairline and the opaque plates: the panel still reads, it just stops feeling like it floats.

Sources

A source is one folder, and that is the whole visible control

Everything a source is comes from one string. useSource builds its binding as config.path || manifest.path || "" (source.js:38), the host turns that into a slot whose writable is simply Boolean(folderPath) (host.js:64), and a created note lands at <folderPath>/<title>.md (host.js:125). Reading and writing are the same path because they are the same field.

Sort and window

Why a filter can be lockedfrom the manifest

A row marked Fixed is the widget's own declaration, and source.js refuses an edit to it and returns a reason ({ reason: "fixed" }). The kanban's board is @board is one of these: unfix it and the board tabs stop working. So the panel shows it rather than hiding it, and shows it as a plain row with no chevron rather than as a control that silently fails.

Why the @ rows read as sentencesa call I made

In the file these are { prop: "board", op: "is", value: "@board" } and { spread: "@filters" }. Drawn literally, the second is unreadable to anyone who has not read resolveFilter. So each row is a sentence with its raw form beneath it in the row's own sub‑line — the sentence for reading, the mono so it is still findable in the board file.

Window is not persisted todayhonest gap

useSource holds the window in useState and nothing writes it to the tile. Drawn here because the tab is where it belongs; building it means one more key in sources[name] alongside filters and sort.

Actions

Not a second list. What this source can already do

There is no action to configure. For every source the engine derives a matching set — create, update, open, and the three can… flags that gate them (surface.js:267–276) — and all three flags are the same boolean: whether the folder path is non‑empty. So the panel does not offer a list to edit. It reports, in the same grouped list as everything else, and it reports one path's consequences.

This is the whole "not set" story for actions: one empty field turns the rows grey, and the reason is the row above them, not a separate diagnosis.

Should a source be able to write somewhere other than it reads? My answer is no, and the cost is specific. A second writePath would buy exactly one verb. update and remove act on a ref.path that came off a row the source already listed, so they can only ever touch the read folder — a write path is meaningless to them. Only create could honour it, and then it would break its own feedback: the folder watcher subscribes with file.path.startsWith(folderPath), so a note created elsewhere never comes back through the list and pressing "New task" would visibly do nothing. If a widget really needs a second destination, that destination is a second source with its own path — the manifest's sources is already a map, so this costs one manifest key and no new concept.

Slots

A fixed set, and an open list

Two shapes, and the difference is not cosmetic. A slot is a template hole: the parent resolves the data and hands it down, so the child has no sources and no settings of its own — only its identity is configurable. A mount is a nested tile minus geometry: it goes through its own WidgetHost, resolves its own sources and keeps its own settings. The panel offers different things for the two, and both are one grouped list.

Nesting

The window never opens a second window. It re‑roots.

This is the answer to the regress. There is exactly one settings window on screen, ever. Pressing the arrow on a slot or a mount does not open a child panel — it swaps what the canvas is showing and pushes one crumb into the floating header. Going back is pressing the crumb. A panel inside a panel cannot happen because the second panel does not exist.

The hard question

Several sources, one folder, one place to change it

You reached for a constant — one endpoint list, reused everywhere. Before designing that, the fear has to be split, because half of it the architecture already answers and half of it is real.

Not real — the folder in three places inside one widgetAnswered

The worry was that a source needs a folder, then the action needs one, then the slot needs one. None of that is true. Actions are derived from the source and share its single boolean; a slotted child has no sources at all. Changing the kanban's folder changes what it reads, where a new task is created, whether the create button is even enabled, and what every card in it is drawn from — from one field. The design's job here is only to show that, which is why the Actions rows report and do not edit.

Real — several widgets on one board over one folderNeeds a mechanism

Count it on the board this page draws. The board tabs declare a tasks source at Orbitask/Tasks. The filter panel declares one. The kanban declares one, and it is mounted, so it resolves it through its own host rather than inheriting anything. That is three separate strings holding the same fact, and a mount holder with five views would be seven. Move the folder and the board half‑breaks, quietly, in whichever widget you forgot.

BOARD · Widgetarium tile board-tabs source tasks → Orbitask/Tasks tile filter-panel source tasks → Orbitask/Tasks tile page-header no sources tile view-group no sources of its own ├──→ mount kanban-board source tasks → Orbitask/Tasks └──→ mount archived-columns no sources └──→ slot task-card handed its row — no source, ever three strings, one fact — and the slot contributes none, which is why slots are not part of this problem

A board‑level named pathTake this

The board file grows a sources table beside the context table it already has, and a source's path may be a name: @tasks. One string, one edit, every reader follows. Cost: one resolution step in useSource, and a second place to look when a path is wrong — paid off by showing the resolved folder under the name, always.

The outer widget passes it downNo

It fails twice. It contradicts what a mount is — resolving its own sources through its own host is the entire reason mounts exist rather than slots — so it would turn every mount into a slot. And it does not even cover the case: the board tabs and the filter panel are siblings of the group, not children of it, so two of the three strings are out of its reach.

Reuse context for itNo

Tempting, because the @ indirection already exists there and resolveFilter already substitutes it. But context is a runtime selection with a single owning widget, subscribed to and re‑rendered on change. A folder is not a selection: nothing should be able to write it while the board is running, and no widget should own it. Same syntax, different table, resolved when the source binds rather than every frame.

And the constant is never something you have to learn first. Every path row stays a plain folder picker. The panel simply tells the truth it already knows — "3 widgets on this board read this folder" — and offers Link them. Pressing it creates the named path, rewrites all three to point at the name, and from then on each row shows the name with the real folder in its sub‑line. Unlink writes the literal path back everywhere and the name disappears. Nobody is asked to design an endpoint list up front; you get one by pressing the sentence that was already telling you the problem.

Three things the model does not carry yet

Found while reading the code this page draws an interface for. None is large; all three are load‑bearing for the window above.

A slot's choice is never savedresolveSlots reads tile.slots?.[name], but normalizeTile does not normalise a slots key and serializeBoard does not write one. So the Slots row above can be pressed, will work until the board is saved, and will then come back as the widget's default. One key in each of the two functions, alongside settings and sources.
The window is state, not settingsuseSource keeps offset and limit in useState, seeded from the manifest. The Data tab draws them as settings, which means they need a home in sources[name] beside filters and sort.
An open mount list wants an array, not a stringThe views setting is a comma‑separated text field today, and idsOf already accepts an array as well as a string. Reordering by dragging writes an array; the string form stays readable for a hand‑edited board, so both paths keep working with no migration.

Calls I made that you did not specify

Each of these is a decision the brief left open. They are drawn above rather than left blank, so the page is honest about what it proposes — and any of them can go the other way without touching the rest.

The tabs are Settings / Data / DesignYou proposed Default / Advanced / Design. "Default" and "Advanced" name a difficulty, and the split is not difficulty — it is one path everybody touches versus the query behind it that almost nobody does. Your words fit the same three tabs unchanged if you prefer them.
Actions have no tab of their ownThey live in the Data tab as a grouped list under the source that produced them. Giving them a tab would say they are a thing you configure, which is the exact belief the code contradicts.
Headings sit outside their blockThe macOS System Settings pattern: small muted text above the filled group, the group holding rows only. It removed the divider that used to sit under a heading — a line that existed only to repair a structure that should not have been built that way — and most of the other lines with it.
Hairlines are opt-in, not defaultOnly a genuine list of like rows gets them: the four abilities, the filter clauses, the views, the board paths. A group of two or three unlike controls gets none. If that ever makes two rows hard to tell apart, the answer is a different fill, not the line back.
The value pickers are the filter panel's popoverPress a row and a panel takes its place with the choices in it, a keyword field, and Reset / Apply — the shape widgets/@orbitask/filter-panel already ships, on the kit's Popover. The earlier inline expansion is gone, and with it the row that changed material when it opened.
The header is a capsule, not a full-width barSame blur, same hairline, less canvas taken. macOS can afford the full width because its content scrolls on one axis past a fixed top edge; this canvas pans on two and has no such edge. Widening it back is one property.
Exactly one edge exists, on the panelLaw one is fill, never outline. An earlier draft of this page had the panel outlined, each group inside it outlined, and rows divided inside those — three edges stacked. All of them are gone except --wg-glass-edge on the outermost floating panel, which a translucent surface over moving content genuinely needs. Where a border was doing the separating, the fix was the fill underneath it, not a thinner line.
A group on glass gets a new tokenThe kit had no fill calibrated against a translucent surface. --wg-glass-group is color-mix(--wg-ink 6%, --wg-raise) — a step toward the ink from the panel's own colour, so it holds in both themes and over any canvas. It belongs in styles.css beside the other three glass tokens if this is approved. The percentage is the arguable part; that it must step from the ink and not from a background is not.
Text never sits on the glassThe floating panels are glass; every grouped list inside them is opaque --wg-raise. The arithmetic is in the glass section: a 78% tint over a glyph is a 1.55:1 ghost, fine behind a 14 px label, dirty behind an 11 px one.
The header stops short of the sidebarTwo floating panels that tile the top‑right corner, rather than one bar running the full width behind the other. The Apple Maps alternative — a small title capsule left, a controls capsule right — is equally defensible and gives back more canvas.
The legibility floor is 0.7You did not name one, and the fit arithmetic needs one or a wide widget opens at a scale nobody can read. 0.7 puts the kit's smallest 12 px type at 8.4 px. Below it the window starts panned instead, and says so in the bar. The number is arguable; that there must be one is not.
The canvas is look‑only below 1:1The strongest call on the page. It costs the ability to press a control in a zoomed‑out widget; it buys a canvas where popovers, blurs and touch targets are never broken, and makes "press to go 1:1" one obvious gesture rather than a mode to find.
The pan clamp is one cell, stated against the panelsNot against the window: a widget hanging off the left edge is still visible, one under an opaque panel is not. One cell is the smallest unit the board itself thinks in, so it needs no new number.
The sidebar folds to a capsuleAn escape that costs one control in the zoom bar. Without it there is no way to see the whole of a widget that exactly fills the free area, and folding also grows the free area so Fit recomputes.
Remove stays on the tile, not in the headerRemoving acts on the board, not on the configuration, and a bin beside a Done control in one small capsule is the one mis‑press you cannot undo. If it must be here it goes alone at the foot of the last tab.
The phone gets a bottom sheet, not a sidebarA right‑hand panel and a 375 px screen cannot both exist. The idea survives — one canvas, floating chrome — but the second panel moves to where a thumb is, the free area is measured against the sheet's peek height, and the clamp turns vertical.
The grow is 320 ms on the springThe tile transition is 170 ms on a private curve today, tuned for a resize of a few cells. This box crosses the board, so it takes --wg-dur-m and --wg-spring — and closing takes --wg-ease, per the kit's rule about panels that bounce shut.
"Link them" is offered, never requiredThe alternative was to make board paths a first‑class thing you set up before adding widgets — a concept tax charged to everybody to solve a problem some boards never have. The promotion gesture charges only the boards that have it.
Board paths are per boardNot per vault. A board is the unit that gets copied and shared, and a vault‑wide constant would make a copied board depend on something invisible that travelled with it.
Uppercase captions are goneYou asked for it and I took it further than the three you named: every mono, tracked, uppercase label on this page — section eyebrows and popover headers included — is plain sentence case in the interface font. Nothing is shouted.
The second copy of a mounted widget is labelledresolveMounts makes it …#2 and it keeps its own settings. Two identical rows would be a trap, so the copy carries a marker. What it should say instead of "2nd copy" is yours — it could equally be the view's own name once views are nameable.
