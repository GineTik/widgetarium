Dark Ramp Fill Trials

The kit fills every control with --color-base-20. In light that is one step down from the note and reads as a raised chip. Under the dark ramp this project recorded, base-20 is --background-secondary — the chrome behind the note, and darker than it — so the same control reads as a hole punched through the page. Five candidate fills, three grounds at once, measured.

Defect

The base ramp is authored by the theme, and its dark shape is not guaranteed — Obsidian's own dark defaults rise cleanly, the ramp recorded in this repo reverses at base-20. A fill picked by position on that ramp is a bet on a stranger's file. A fill defined by relationship to the surface is not.

Three ramps, two shapes

Plotted in CIE L*, the perceptual lightness of each step. Obsidian's light defaults descend without turning back, and its dark defaults rise without turning back — stock, the ramp is well-formed and base-20 is a perfectly good fill. The third is the dark ramp recorded in this repo's own reference pages, and it reverses: base-20 drops below base-00, because there it is --background-secondary, the sidebar and tab-header colour, which sits behind the note rather than on it.

That is the whole problem in one sentence. The kit cannot see which of these two shapes a reader's vault ships, so it must not depend on the answer.

Two invariants, both computed

The fill steps toward the ink — darker than a white note, lighter than a dark one. That is the strip's pass/fail, and it compares the sign of fill − surface against the sign of ink − surface.

A raised surface lifts. A card on its plate, a thumb on its track and a count chip all have to be lighter than what they sit on, on every ground — that is what raised looks like. Each readout says lifts or sinks, measured the same way.

Why a ratio and not a screenshot

These separations are all near 1:1 — the working range for a surface step is roughly 1.10 to 1.30. Below about 1.05 the edge disappears on a dim laptop panel whatever the swatch looks like here, so the number decides and the picture confirms.

The last readout is the three-level check: note, plate, card. A 1.000 there means the card is painted the note's own colour — legible only because the plate lies between them, and a warning that the stack has two colours, not three.

Five fills, one specimen set

Each strip renders the identical set — a plate holding a card, three capsule buttons, an icon button, a segmented control with its thumb, a pill, a count chip, a search field and a progress bar — under one fill, on all three grounds at once. The Obsidian variables are scoped to a class, not to a media query, so nothing here depends on your OS setting.

Choosing N

How far color-mix should travel toward the ink. Too small and the control vanishes in dark; too large and the light theme grows a grey the design never asked for. The band that clears 1.10 in both themes without exceeding light's current base-25 is the answer.

The pick

E — paired relationship tokens

The fill steps toward the ink, so it cannot invert on any ramp. The raised token steps toward the light, so a card and a thumb stay above the fill instead of falling back to the note's own colour. B, C and D fix the fill and leave the second defect untouched: --background-primary is both the note and the raised surface, so on a dark ground the card and the thumb come out darker than the plate they sit on — elevation reads backwards even though the ratio is fine. E is the only one where the note / plate / card stack is three colours instead of two.

If only one line may change, take D: it fixes the fill with no new token and no ramp step to re-pick when a theme moves. B is a weaker step than D on all three grounds and is still a ramp position; C is semantically the best name and the least bounded value, since Obsidian does not publish a default for it and a theme may point it anywhere. A is the only candidate that actually inverts, and it does so only under a theme's ramp — which is exactly why the kit should stop asking.

The change, in styles.css

/* a fill is a step from the surface toward the ink,
   never a position on a ramp that does not mirror */
- --wg-kit-fill: var(--color-base-20, var(--background-secondary));
- --wg-kit-fill-hover: var(--color-base-25, var(--background-modifier-hover));
+ --wg-kit-fill: color-mix(in srgb, var(--text-normal) 7%, var(--background-primary));
+ --wg-kit-fill-hover: color-mix(in srgb, var(--text-normal) 12%, var(--background-primary));
/* and elevation is light in both themes, so a raised
   surface lifts instead of returning to the note */
+ --wg-kit-raise: color-mix(in srgb, #ffffff 12%, var(--background-primary));

  then .wg-kit-card, .wg-kit-seg-thumb and .wg-kit-count
  take --wg-kit-raise where they take --background-primary today.

What the numbers changed about the argument

01

Stock Obsidian is not broken. Its dark ramp rises cleanly — base-00 #1c1c1c through base-30 #333333 — so base-20 is a lighter, perfectly readable fill there. The hole appears only under a theme that points base-20 at its chrome colour, which is what the recorded ramp does. The bug is a dependency, not a colour.

02

base-20 is not invisible by ratio. On the recorded dark ground it measures against the note — close to base-10's. What fails is direction, not separation: it steps away from the ink, so the control recesses instead of rising.

03

The raised surfaces are already wrong, today. Wherever the fill is lighter than the note, a thumb painted --background-primary becomes the darkest thing in the control — which the shipping kit does on stock Obsidian right now, and which B, C and D each reproduce on both dark grounds. The card on its plate and the count chip are the same defect twice more. Only E moves the raised token with the fill.

Which values are whose

The light ramp in orbitask-converted.html is Obsidian's real default, verbatim — #ffffff · #fcfcfc · #fafafa · #f6f6f6 · #efefef · #e4e4e4, and base-40/50/60/100 match too. widgetarium-ui-kit.html carries a different light ramp (#f5f6f8 / #ededf0) that is somebody's approximation.

The dark ramp both files record is not Obsidian's. Obsidian ships #1c1c1c · #212121 · #232323 · #282828 · #2e2e2e · #333333 — neutral grey and monotonic. The recorded #1b1c20 … #14151a is blue-biased and reversed: a theme's ramp, labelled in both files as "Obsidian's own defaults". Anything reasoned from that label alone is reasoning about one theme.
