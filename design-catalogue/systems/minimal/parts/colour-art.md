# Colour: art

The interface stays monochrome; colour lives in a few **art objects** that set the mood. The page still has almost no cards and no colourful controls. Seen in AI products and product pages that explain themselves.

- **An art object** is a picture-like thing that carries colour on its own: a soft gradient sphere or field, a textured colour panel behind two key cards, an illustration-like product visual, a coloured photo.
- **At most one art object per viewport** (what fits on the person's screen), not per page. Scrolling reveals them one at a time.
- **Controls never take the art colours.** Buttons stay ink, links stay ink or the pen, tags stay grey.
- **Art sits behind or beside the content, never under text that must be read;** text over art stands on a white card or in a solid band.
- **Art objects are found or generated as files, never drawn by hand in code** (`design-catalogue/README.md`): a gradient field may be CSS; a sphere, an illustration or a photo is a file or a labelled placeholder.
- **The same object may return** (a sphere in the hero, a smaller one in a section), so the product has one recognisable visual.

Pick it for product pages, onboarding, AI products and the first screen of an app that needs to feel alive. Not for screens of tables and records: there, use monochrome or spot.
